Problem

Traceability breaks
when AI writes along

A GMP document has to show who created or changed what, when and why. When someone drafts a root cause analysis with ChatGPT or Copilot, three things go missing from the record.

AI use goes unrecorded

When text comes from a chat window or an AI assistant, the final document often shows no trace of it.

The origin of text is unclear

It is often impossible to tell which parts a person wrote, which came from AI, and which were written together.

The decision is unclear

Did the person leading the investigation choose the root cause, or accept the first AI suggestion without checking it?

Many companies hold back on AI in GMP documentation. Where it is used, it is often hard to show afterwards which parts came from AI and who decided.

Why now

The rules ask for traceability.
Regulators are paying attention to AI.

RequirementWhat it means for AI use
ALCOA+, EU GMP Annex 11, 21 CFR Part 11123Records must show who (a person or a system) created or changed what, and when. EU GMP also asks why GMP-relevant data was changed. Once AI is involved, these questions become harder to answer.
Quality unit responsibilityThe quality unit approves or rejects procedures, specifications, materials and products, and GMP documents are approved by authorised people. AI can support this work, but the company stays accountable for the integrity of records produced with AI.456
EU GMP Annex 22 (draft)The first EU GMP annex dedicated to AI, published as a draft in July 2025 and not yet final. The draft keeps generative AI out of critical GMP uses, but the EMA is now reconsidering this and has discussed allowing it under a documented, risk-based control strategy.78
FDA enforcementIn April 2026 the FDA cited a drug manufacturer whose quality unit had not reviewed specifications, procedures and master production records created with AI. The FDA stated that AI output or recommendations must be reviewed and cleared by an authorised person in the quality unit.4
InspectionsAn HPRA GMP inspector said at the 2026 QP Forum in Dublin that inspectors may ask at the opening meeting whether AI is used in any GMP system or process.9

No rule yet requires labelling AI-written text. But if you use AI in GMP documentation today, be ready to show an inspector where AI was used and how people reviewed and approved the result.

How it works

How DiafaneAI
will make AI use traceable

This is what we are building. Which parts come first depends on what we learn from QA teams.

Step 1 · Record

Capture AI use where people already work

DiafaneAI is designed to capture AI use where people already work. Planned integrations include Microsoft Word and Copilot, AI chats such as ChatGPT, and eQMS platforms such as Veeva Vault, MasterControl and TrackWise Digital. It should work without extra clicks, and users are informed and can see what is recorded.

AI interactions in connected tools are logged with person, time, action and document section, in a secure audit trail that users cannot edit or switch off. Each section shows whether it was written by a person, came from a recorded AI suggestion, or was created by a person and AI together. AI use outside connected tools cannot be detected automatically.

Step 2 · Decide

Show who made the decision

Inspectors hold people, not tools, responsible for GMP decisions. DiafaneAI will keep the prompt and the options the AI suggested, and record which one a person chose and which they rejected.

Edits to AI text are captured. The final approval is designed to use an electronic signature that meets 21 CFR Part 11 and EU GMP Annex 11, in DiafaneAI or in your existing eQMS. As with any GMP system, DiafaneAI will need to be validated for its intended use.

Later

Inspection report

One report per document for inspectors: what came from AI, who decided and who approved. Detailed prompts are not part of the standard report but remain available on request, under your data-integrity rules.
